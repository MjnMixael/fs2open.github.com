Checkpoint Options

Opens via Editors › Mission Specs › Checkpoints.

Mission-wide settings for the in-engine checkpoint system. A checkpoint saves the state of a mission in progress so the player can resume from it later, including after quitting the game.

Checkpoints are written by the store-checkpoint operator, so a mission that never calls it has none. These settings control what happens around that.

What a checkpoint remembers

Enough that a resumed mission is the mission you left, not a fresh copy of it:

  • Ships — class, team, position, hull, shields, every subsystem, weapon banks and ammunition, countermeasures, cargo, flags, and physics.
  • Who is left — ships destroyed, departed or not yet arrived, wing waves and their timers, and the mission log exactly as it read.
  • Mission logic — events and their repeat counts, goals, SEXP variables and containers, and the settled state of anything that had resolved for good, including numbers rand had already rolled.
  • Orders and docking — every AI order and target, what each ship was guarding, chasing, ignoring or flying towards, which ships were docked to which, and at which bays.
  • The battlefield — capital ship wreckage, model animations such as open bay doors and deployed turrets, and changes a mission made to ships that had not arrived yet.
  • The world — skybox, background bitmaps and suns, ambient light, nebula, soundtrack, HUD toggles, jump nodes and navpoints.
  • The player's context — score, hotkey assignments, the escort list, support ship calls used and the rearm stockpile spent, built-in message allowances, and the training directive context.
  • Scripts — whatever a script asked to have remembered. See On Checkpoint Save, On Checkpoint Restore and mission.setCheckpointData().

What it deliberately does not

These are decisions, not gaps. Each one either lasts a second or two, or cannot be reproduced without doing more harm than the loss:

  • Weapons and beams in flight. The shots that were in the air are gone, but turret firing cadence resumes, so a restored battle picks straight back up.
  • Shockwaves, particles, decals, trails, sparks and looping sounds.
  • Small debris. Capital ship wreckage is kept, because it is cover.
  • The camera, cutscene bars, fades and subtitles. A checkpoint taken mid-fade resumes with a clear screen rather than a black one.
  • Queued dialogue that had not been said yet, and an asteroid field that a mission rebuilt mid-flight — the field the mission file describes comes back either way.
  • Autopilot, which is disengaged on resume rather than picked up half way.
  • A ship in the middle of its death roll, which is recorded as already destroyed.

Availability

ControlDescription
Disallow checkpoints in campaign modeEvery checkpoint operator does nothing while this mission is flown as part of a campaign, and no resume prompt appears.
Disallow checkpoints in simulator modeThe same, for when the mission is flown outside a campaign — from the mission simulator, or launched directly.

These are separate switches because the two contexts often want different behavior: a checkpoint is a courtesy during a long campaign mission, but it can undermine a mission being replayed for score. Checking both switches checkpoints off entirely for this mission.

Resuming

When a usable checkpoint exists, the engine asks the player whether to resume on the way into the mission — after the briefing and loadout, before the first frame, with no second loading screen. These settings control that prompt.

ControlDescription
Do not offer to resume on mission entrySuppresses the automatic prompt. The checkpoint still exists and load-checkpoint still works; use this for missions where a scripted mid-mission prompt is the intended flow.
Keep the player's briefing loadout when resumingLeaves the player's ship class and weapons as the briefing set them, rather than restoring the ones the checkpoint recorded. Lets the player retry with a different fit.
Keep the player wing's briefing loadout when resumingThe same, for the rest of the player's wing.

The two loadout options apply only to the automatic prompt, which is why they are unavailable once it is suppressed. load-checkpoint and prompt-user-checkpoint-load take the same options as arguments of their own.

Lifetime

ControlDescription
Delete this mission's checkpoints once it is completedDiscards them when the player finishes the mission successfully, so replaying it later starts clean instead of offering a checkpoint from the previous run. A failed mission keeps its checkpoints, since that is exactly when they are wanted.

Without this, checkpoints persist until delete-checkpoint is called or a later store-checkpoint overwrites the slot.

Notes

  • Checkpoints are stored per pilot, per campaign and per mission, so one pilot's checkpoints never appear for another.
  • A checkpoint saved before the mission file was last edited is treated as absent, because the mission's events and variables would no longer line up with it.
  • Checkpoints do nothing in multiplayer.
  • Scripts can attach their own data to a checkpoint with mission.setCheckpointData() and read it back from the On Checkpoint Restore hook, which runs once the mission is fully restored.
