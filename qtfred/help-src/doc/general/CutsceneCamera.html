Cutscene Camera Preview

While a camera SEXP is selected in a SEXP tree (usually in the Events editor), the viewport shows where that camera ends up. Select the operator itself or any of its arguments. Nothing here changes how the mission plays; it only helps you place the camera.

What is shown

The camera is drawn as a cyan frustum: a pyramid pointing where the camera looks, as wide as its field of view, with a small arrow on top so you can see which way is up. A darker line runs to what the camera faces (a set-camera-facing point, a set-camera-facing-object object, or a set-camera-target target), and a green line runs to its host (set-camera-host).

QtFRED works the camera out the way the game does, running the camera SEXPs in order up to and including the selected one. These are understood:

  • set-camera (switches between named cameras, each keeping its own state)
  • set-camera-position, set-camera-rotation, set-camera-facing, set-camera-facing-object, with their move times
  • set-camera-host, set-camera-target (positions are relative to the host, and a target turns the camera toward it)
  • set-camera-fov

A number argument that is a variable uses the variable's starting value, and an argument that is an operator (such as +) makes QtFRED skip that SEXP.

Where the camera starts: "Starts after"

A cutscene is usually spread over several events, one shot each, and each shot carries on from where the last one left the camera. So the preview first runs the camera SEXPs of the event this one starts after, and of the one that starts after, and so on.

The Starts after box on the toolbar (shown while looking through the camera) chooses that event:

  • Automatic works it out: a chained event starts after the event before it in the list, and otherwise an event starts after an event its formula waits for with is-event-true, is-event-true-delay or is-event-true-msecs-delay. Only events with camera SEXPs count. The event it picked is shown in brackets.
  • New camera starts from a new camera at the origin.
  • Or pick any event with camera SEXPs.

A choice is saved with the mission for the editor only (the game ignores it), and follows the event if it is renamed. If the chosen event is deleted or loses its camera SEXPs, the box shows it as (missing), the preview goes back to automatic, and the Error Checker warns about it. Changing it can be undone in the Events editor.

Looking through the camera

View › Viewpoint › Cutscene Camera, Look Through Camera in the SEXP tree's right-click menu, or Ctrl+Shift+V shows the viewport through the selected camera at its field of view (the set-camera-fov value, or the in-game FOV). Ctrl+Shift+V cycles the editor camera, the current ship and the cutscene camera, skipping the ones there is nothing to look through. Selecting a different camera SEXP updates the view, so you can step through a cutscene shot by shot. The view goes back to the editor camera when no camera SEXP is selected or the dialog is closed.

Flying the camera

While looking through the camera, the usual movement keys (and a SpaceMouse) fly it, at the Camera Move and Camera Rot speeds on the toolbar. When you stop, the event's camera SEXPs take the new place, as one step in the Events editor's undo history: its set-camera-position takes the position (relative to the host, if the camera has one), and its set-camera-rotation or set-camera-facing takes the direction. The camera only moves the ways the event can store: with no position SEXP it can only turn, and with no rotation or facing SEXP it can only move. The arguments must be plain numbers.

Playing a shot

The toolbar's |<, Play/Pause and >| buttons play the selected event's shot. All of an event's camera SEXPs start at once, as they do in the game, and each move eases in and out the way the game moves it. The time readout shows how far into the shot the camera is. The frustum plays too, so a shot can be watched from outside with the editor camera. Stopped, the preview shows the selected SEXP's end again; selecting another camera SEXP stops playback.

Moving the camera in the viewport

When the selected SEXP is a set-camera-position or set-camera-facing with plain number arguments, the point it sets gets a cyan handle in the viewport. In Move mode, drag the handle to move the camera (or the point it faces). The arguments are written when you let go, as one step in the dialog's undo history. Right-click or press Escape during the drag to cancel it. The drag follows the usual constraint plane and the toolbar's axis locks.

Setting the camera from the viewport

Fly the editor camera to the shot you want, then use View › Viewpoint › Set Camera SEXP from View (also in the viewport's right-click menu) or Set from Viewport in the SEXP tree's right-click menu. The event's camera SEXPs take the view, as when flying the camera: its set-camera-position the position and its set-camera-rotation or set-camera-facing the direction (a facing point is put in the middle of the view, as far away as it was). The editor camera uses a narrower field of view than the game, so look through the camera afterwards to check the framing, or fly the camera itself instead.

Showing and hiding

View › Show Cutscene Camera turns the frustum and handle on or off. Looking through the camera still works while they are hidden.
