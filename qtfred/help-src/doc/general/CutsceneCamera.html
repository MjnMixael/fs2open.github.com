Cutscene Camera Preview

While a camera SEXP is selected in a SEXP tree (usually in the Events editor), the viewport shows where that camera ends up. Select the operator itself or any of its arguments. Nothing here is saved to the mission; it only helps you place the camera.

What is shown

The camera is drawn as a cyan frustum: a pyramid pointing where the camera looks, as wide as its field of view, with a small arrow on top so you can see which way is up. A darker line runs to what the camera faces (a set-camera-facing point, a set-camera-facing-object object, or a set-camera-target target), and a green line runs to its host (set-camera-host).

To work out the camera, QtFRED runs the camera SEXPs of the same event in order, up to and including the selected one. These are understood:

  • set-camera (switches between named cameras, each keeping its own state)
  • set-camera-position, set-camera-rotation, set-camera-facing, set-camera-facing-object
  • set-camera-host, set-camera-target (positions are relative to the host, and a target turns the camera toward it)
  • set-camera-fov

The preview shows where each move ends, not the transition. Each event starts from a new camera at the origin, since QtFRED can't know which earlier events will have run. A number argument that is a variable uses the variable's starting value, and an argument that is an operator (such as +) makes QtFRED skip that SEXP.

Moving the camera in the viewport

When the selected SEXP is a set-camera-position or set-camera-facing with plain number arguments, the point it sets gets a cyan handle in the viewport. In Move mode, drag the handle to move the camera (or the point it faces). The arguments are written when you let go, as one step in the dialog's undo history. Right-click or press Escape during the drag to cancel it. The drag follows the usual constraint plane and the toolbar's axis locks.

Looking through the camera

View › Viewpoint › Cutscene Camera, or Look Through Camera in the SEXP tree's right-click menu, shows the viewport through the selected camera at its field of view (the set-camera-fov value, or the in-game FOV). Selecting a different camera SEXP updates the view, so you can step through a cutscene shot by shot. The camera controls don't move the view while you look through it. Switch back with Viewpoint › Camera; the view also goes back to the editor camera when no camera SEXP is selected or the dialog is closed.

Setting a camera SEXP from the viewport

Fly the editor camera to the shot you want, then use View › Viewpoint › Set Camera SEXP from View (also in the viewport's right-click menu) or Set from Viewport in the SEXP tree's right-click menu. What is written depends on the selected SEXP:

  • set-camera-position takes the view's position (relative to the host, if the camera has one).
  • set-camera-rotation takes the view's orientation (relative to the host's orientation or the target's facing, if the camera has one).
  • set-camera-facing takes the point in the middle of the view, at the same distance as its current point.

The editor camera uses a narrower field of view than the game, so look through the camera afterwards to check the framing. The arguments must be plain numbers. The change can be undone in the dialog.

Showing and hiding

View › Show Cutscene Camera turns the frustum and handle on or off. Looking through the camera still works while they are hidden.
