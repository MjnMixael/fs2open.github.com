Cutscene Camera Preview

While a camera SEXP is selected in a SEXP tree (usually in the Events editor), the viewport shows the cutscene camera, and can play the shot it is part of. Select the operator itself or any of its arguments. Nothing here changes how the mission plays; it only helps you place the camera.

The preview follows the rules below. Cutscenes written the way they describe preview exactly as the game plays them; anything outside them may preview differently (see Outside the rules).

The rules

  1. An event is one shot. All of an event's camera SEXPs run at the same moment, as they do in the game. SEXPs without a time set the start of the shot; SEXPs with a time move it from there to the end, easing in and out the way the game moves it. When the same thing (position, direction, field of view) is set more than once, the last one wins, as in the game.
  2. A shot starts where the shot of its "Starts after" event ended. See Starts after. With no such event, the shot starts from a new camera at the origin. The preview assumes each earlier shot has finished before the next one starts.
  3. What is shown depends on what is selected. Selecting a SEXP without a time shows the start of the shot; selecting a timed one shows its end. Play runs the whole shot from start to end.
  4. Editing only ever changes the selected SEXP, and only the part it holds: a position moves, a rotation or facing turns, a field of view zooms.

These camera SEXPs are understood: set-camera (switches between named cameras, each keeping its own state), set-camera-position, set-camera-rotation, set-camera-facing, set-camera-facing-object, set-camera-host (positions are relative to the host), set-camera-target (turns the camera toward the target) and set-camera-fov.

One thing the game does that may surprise you: a set-camera-facing aims from where the camera is when the SEXP runs, which is the start of the shot. If the same shot also moves the camera, it ends up pointing the way it was aimed from the start, not necessarily straight at the point. The preview shows it the same way.

Outside the rules

The preview doesn't try to handle these, and may differ from the game:

  • Camera SEXPs that might not run, such as those in if-then-else branches or behind conditions: the preview runs all of them.
  • Repeating and every-time events, and shots that overlap because the next one starts before the last one finishes.
  • An order of shots that neither chaining nor is-event-true describes. Set Starts after by hand for those.
  • Arguments that are variables (their starting value is used) or operators such as + (that SEXP is skipped).

What is shown

The camera is drawn as a cyan frustum: a pyramid pointing where the camera looks, as wide as its field of view, with a small arrow on top so you can see which way is up. A darker line runs to what the camera faces (a set-camera-facing point, a set-camera-facing-object object, or a set-camera-target target), and a green line runs to its host (set-camera-host).

When a later SEXP of the same shot overrides what the selected one does (for example, a set-camera-rotation right after a set-camera-facing, both without a time), a faint ghost frustum shows the selected SEXP's own pose, the camera as it leaves it. The game shows the bright one; the ghost is what the selected SEXP would do if nothing replaced it. Dragging its handle moves the ghost.

Looking through the camera

View › Viewpoint › Cutscene Camera, Look Through Camera in the SEXP tree's right-click menu, or Ctrl+Shift+V shows the viewport through the camera at its field of view: the last set-camera-fov, including one from an earlier shot, or else the in-game FOV. Ctrl+Shift+V cycles the editor camera, the current ship and the cutscene camera, skipping the ones there is nothing to look through. Selecting a different camera SEXP updates the view, so you can step through a cutscene shot by shot. The view goes back to the editor camera when no camera SEXP is selected or the dialog is closed.

While looking through the camera the toolbar shows the playback buttons and Starts after, and its FOV box shows the camera's field of view. The box can be changed while a set-camera-fov is selected, and writes into it.

Playing a shot

The toolbar's |<, Play/Pause and >| buttons play the selected SEXP's shot, with the time into it beside them. The frustum plays too, so a shot can also be watched from outside with the editor camera. Stopped, the preview goes back to the start or end of the shot (rule 3); selecting another camera SEXP stops playback. Editing waits while the shot is being played or is paused part way through.

Starts after

The Starts after box on the toolbar chooses the event whose shot this one starts from:

  • Automatic works it out: a chained event starts after the event before it in the list, and otherwise an event starts after an event its formula waits for with is-event-true, is-event-true-delay or is-event-true-msecs-delay. Only events with camera SEXPs count. The event it picked is shown in brackets.
  • New camera starts from a new camera at the origin.
  • Or pick any event with camera SEXPs.

A choice is saved with the mission for the editor only (the game ignores it), and follows the event if it is renamed. If the chosen event is deleted or loses its camera SEXPs, the box shows it as (missing), the preview goes back to automatic, and the Error Checker warns about it. Changing it can be undone in the Events editor.

Editing the selected SEXP

These only ever change the selected SEXP, and need its arguments to be plain numbers. Each change is one step in the dialog's undo history.

  • Dragging: a selected set-camera-position or set-camera-facing puts a cyan handle on its point. In Move mode, drag it to move the camera or the point it faces. The arguments are written when you let go; right-click or press Escape during the drag to cancel it. The drag follows the usual constraint plane and the toolbar's axis locks.
  • Flying: while looking through the camera, the usual movement keys (and a SpaceMouse) fly it at the Camera Move and Camera Rot speeds, but only the way the selected SEXP can take: a set-camera-position moves, a set-camera-rotation or set-camera-facing turns, and anything else stays put. When you stop, the SEXP takes the new place: a position (relative to the host, if the camera has one), a rotation's angles, or a facing point straight ahead, as far away as it was, so the camera faces the way you turned it.
  • Set from the viewport: fly the editor camera to the view you want, then use View › Viewpoint › Set Camera SEXP from View (also in the viewport's right-click menu) or Set from Viewport in the SEXP tree's right-click menu. The selected SEXP takes the view the same way: a position takes where the view is, and a rotation or facing the way it looks. The editor camera uses a narrower field of view than the game, so look through the camera afterwards to check the framing.

Showing and hiding

View › Show Cutscene Camera turns the frustum and handle on or off. Looking through the camera still works while they are hidden.
